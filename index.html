The FYP journey

Six stages, one cycle

Every project moves through the same checkpoints. Track where your batch stands and jump to the page you need.

1

Idea & Group Formation

Pick a domain, form a group of 2–3, choose a topic (FYP-1).

2

Proposal Defense

Present scope and feasibility to the FYP committee.

3

Development & First 4 Chapters

Build under supervision and submit the first 4 chapters to close FYP-1.

4

Complete Documentation

Submit complete documentation before final term to open FYP-2.

5

Final Defense

Present the completed system to internal & external examiners.

6

Result & Re-evaluation

Submit final report, receive grade and status (up to 3 re-evaluations if required).

About FYP

Why the Final Year Project matters

The Final Year Project is the capstone of the undergraduate degree in Computer Science and Software Engineering. It asks each student to take a real problem from question to working system — combining requirements analysis, design, implementation, testing, and professional communication into a single sustained piece of work. Supervisors guide the process, but the project is owned by the group: the choices, the trade-offs, and the final defense are theirs to make and to stand behind.

Beyond the deliverable itself, FYP is where students practice working in a team under deadline pressure, presenting technical decisions to a non-trivial audience, and handling the ethical and professional responsibilities that come with building software other people will use.
